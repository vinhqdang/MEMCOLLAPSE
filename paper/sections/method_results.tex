\paragraph{\ledger{} rescues single-mention claims.}
Against rolling on the same cells, \ledger{} raises single-mention critical accuracy by $+0.315$ (Qwen-1.5B, $B{=}160$, $p<10^{-30}$) and $+0.50$ to $+0.54$ (Qwen-7B, both budgets), and is significant in every domain at $B{=}160$.

\paragraph{When the prompt fix is enough.}
Directly against rolling-imp, \ledger{} gains on single-mention critical claims at Qwen-1.5B ($+0.05$ at $B{=}80$, $+0.28$ at $B{=}160$) and at Qwen-7B with $B{=}80$ ($+0.22$); at Qwen-7B with $B{=}160$ the two are tied ($+0.02$, n.s.).
A capable summariser with room to spare follows the instruction to keep warnings; a weak summariser, or a tight budget, does not.
\ledger{} never loses on the target cell.

\paragraph{Typing the summary is not enough.}
Typed compaction raises critical accuracy overall but mainly for repeated claims: it is the most frequency-biased strategy at both model sizes (slope $+0.28$ and $+0.18$ per doubling at $B{=}160$, vs.\ $+0.09$ and $+0.05$ for \ledger{}).

\paragraph{Ablations.}
Eviction by support reproduces the frequency gradient (slope $+0.20$ to $+0.33$).
The middle tier for decisions matters when the top tier must be evicted (Qwen-7B $B{=}80$: $+0.18$ on single-mention claims over the flat tier) and is neutral otherwise.
Rendering provenance costs budget and lowers accuracy.
