\section*{Limitations}

Our dialogues are generated from templates and fictional banks so that frequency can be manipulated with content held fixed; this control is the point of the design, but meetings in the wild are messier. We partly address this with LLM-naturalised dialogues, where the effect persists at a somewhat smaller size, but only for one model and two domains.
We study open models from 1.5B to 7B parameters, run locally, with 7B-scale models quantised to 4 bits; larger and proprietary models may follow importance instructions better, which our 7B results suggest would narrow the gap between \ledger{} and a well-prompted summariser at loose budgets.
Our probes are multiple-choice questions. The decision probes move from recall to action, but they still offer the right action as an option, whereas an agent acting freely would have to generate it.
Critical versus incidental is defined by us; \ledger{}'s tiers rely on the model's type labels, which are noisy for small models.
Budgets of 80 and 160 words are small by design so that rewriting must discard information.
